Context

What's changed in the last 18 months

AI coding tools went from novelty to necessity. The pressure to adopt is real — but so is the risk of moving too fast.

Capability jump

Models can now handle complex codebases, understand context across files, and generate working solutions — not just snippets.

Competitive pressure

Teams that adopt well are shipping faster. Those that don't may fall behind on product velocity and modernisation.

New risks

Speed without discipline creates bugs at scale. AI can generate code faster than humans can review it safely.

Key insight: The bottleneck has shifted. Coding got faster — but alignment, review, and verification didn't.

Grounding

The reality we're working with

Most organisations don't have greenfield projects. They have mature products, legacy systems, and knowledge trapped in people's heads.

What makes this hard

  • Mature, mission-critical products — breaking changes have real consequences
  • Technical and knowledge debt — "how does this work?" is often unanswered
  • Experienced staff with deep expertise — knowledge often lives in their heads

The opportunity

  • Capture institutional knowledge — as documents, not just memory
  • Reduce dependency on single experts — share context, reduce risk
  • Speed up onboarding — new people get up to speed faster

Key Concept

Prototype mode vs Production mode

Exploration is valuable. But what works for a demo won't work for a release.

🧪 Prototype Mode

  • Fast learning — try things, break things, iterate
  • Rough edges OK — it's a proof of concept
  • Low stakes — failure teaches, doesn't cost

🏭 Production Mode

  • Guardrails matter — checkpoints prevent costly mistakes
  • Evidence required — "it works" needs proof
  • Maintainability — someone else will need to understand this

The Method

A simple, repeatable workflow

Four steps. Every time. Whether it's a bug fix or a major feature.

1. Plan AI drafts, asks questions
2. Approve Human checks scope & risks
3. Build AI implements in small steps
4. Prove Evidence that it works

Why plan first?

Vague requests create rework at machine speed. Get clear before you start.

Why small steps?

Small changes are easy to review, easy to undo, and easier to trust.

Why prove?

"It runs" isn't enough. Tests, checks, and validation reports show it actually works.

Proof Over Promises

What "evidence" actually means

"Done" isn't a feeling. It's a set of checks that passed — and a report that shows they did.

Types of evidence

  • Tests passing — automated checks that prove behaviour
  • Security scans clear — no new vulnerabilities introduced
  • Build succeeds — the code compiles and runs
  • Validation report — a document showing what was tested and results

Why this matters

When AI generates code fast, the risk shifts to review and verification. Evidence is how you stay confident while moving quickly.

Rule of thumb: If you can't explain what was tested and show it passed, it's not done.

Collaboration

How teams work together with AI

This isn't just for developers. Everyone in the delivery process has a role to play.

Mob Elaboration (getting aligned)

A short session where the team defines what to build and why. AI helps structure the conversation and capture decisions.

  • Product defines the goal — outcomes, not just features
  • Tech raises constraints — what's possible, what's risky
  • QA defines success — how will we know it works?

Mob Construction (building together)

The team works in short, focused sessions. AI generates, humans review and guide. Decisions happen in real-time.

  • Developer validates code — reviews what AI produces
  • QA checks evidence — are tests covering the right things?
  • ProServ flags real-world issues — "customers will ask about..."

Key point: AI doesn't replace collaboration — it speeds it up. The team still owns decisions. AI just helps capture and execute them faster.

Common Question

Yes, it works on older tech too

AI tools aren't just for shiny new stacks. They can help with legacy systems — with the right approach.

Honest caveats

  • Less training data — AI knows Python better than obscure languages
  • Context is critical — AI needs docs, comments, and examples to work well
  • Verification matters more — less model confidence = more human checking

What helps it work

  • Add documentation — even basic READMEs improve AI output
  • Write tests first — gives AI a clear target to hit
  • Start small — document one module, run one experiment

Bottom line: Don't disqualify yourself because your stack is older. The approach adapts — start with what you have.

✅

📚

The goal: Capture and spread institutional knowledge. AI helps — but humans decide what's important and what's right.

Getting Started

Start small, prove value, then scale

Adoption isn't "flip a switch." It's a practice that builds over time.

1. Pick one thing

Choose a small, bounded backlog item with clear acceptance criteria. Not your biggest project — something contained.

2. Run with evidence

Follow the workflow: plan, approve, build, prove. Capture what worked and what didn't.

3. Share and iterate

Document the win. Update your approach. Try the next item. Build confidence through repetition.
